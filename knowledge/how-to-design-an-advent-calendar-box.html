How to Design an Advent Calendar Box

A step-by-step procurement guide to product mapping, cavity engineering, door sequence, artwork and production for custom calendars.

Direct Answer

To design an Advent calendar box, freeze the complete product assortment, map each item to a numbered cavity, engineer product retention and removal, then approve the structure before final artwork. A packed prototype is essential because one incorrect cavity can affect the whole calendar.

Key Considerations

  • Every item needs dimensions, weight and assigned day.
  • Doors must open without exposing or dislodging adjacent products.
  • The insert must support the calendar in its display orientation.
  • Packing sequence and quality control should be documented.

Engineering Considerations

Map products before drawing doors

Create a day-by-day table with item name, dimensions, weight and orientation. Repeated cavity sizes simplify packing, while mixed assortments need careful spatial planning.

Choose the calendar architecture

Drawer, door, book-style, chest and rotating formats create different interactions. The structure should match product weight, retail display and fulfillment workflow.

Prototype the filled system

Test door perforations or openings, product removal, stability, numbering, packed weight and outer-carton protection. Approve production artwork only after the dieline is stable.

Information required for engineering and quotation

  • Product dimensions, filled weight and presentation orientation
  • Order quantity and destination country
  • Preferred structure, materials and finishes
  • Logo artwork or an AI concept/reference image
  • Required delivery date and packing method

FAQ

When should Advent calendar development start?

Start early enough for product mapping, engineering, sampling, revisions, production and international transport.

Can cavities have different sizes?

Yes, when every item and packing orientation are confirmed before engineering.

Can an Advent calendar be reusable?

It can be designed for reuse, but durability and a credible after-use function must be tested.
